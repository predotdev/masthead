Acme is 40 people in 11 time zones. A meeting time that suits everyone does not exist, and a decision made on a call is invisible to everyone who missed it. So most of our decisions start as a document instead.

## The one-page format

Every proposal answers four questions, in this order:

1. **What are we deciding?** One sentence.
2. **Why now?** What happens if we wait a quarter.
3. **What do we recommend?** With the strongest argument against it, written as its best advocate would write it.
4. **What would change our mind?** The fact that would make this the wrong call.

A page is a limit, not a goal. The best ones are half a page. If a proposal needs more, the extra goes in an appendix that nobody is required to read.

## Comments, then a decision

Anyone can comment for three working days. Comments are questions or objections, not edits. The author then writes the decision at the top, including what they did not take from the comments and why. Nothing is decided in a thread, and nothing is decided by whoever replied last.

## Where documents live

Every decision document goes into one folder, sorted by date, with the decision in the first line. New people read the last twenty on their first day. It is the fastest way we know to learn how a company thinks.

## What we still meet about

Hiring, hard feedback and anything that is mostly about feelings. Writing is good at decisions and bad at those. We also keep one call a week with no agenda at all, because a company needs to hear each other's voices sometimes.

## Try it for a month

If you want to try this, start with one rule: no decision without a page. Everything else here grew from that.
